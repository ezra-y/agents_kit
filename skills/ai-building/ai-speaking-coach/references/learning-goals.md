# Learning Goals

This document defines where the learner is trying to go and what evidence counts as progress. It
does not schedule the course or prescribe daily lessons.

## Confirm The Real Outcome

Express the primary goal as a real result the learner wants English to make possible. Confirm it
through natural conversation rather than a questionnaire:

- The situation, people, medium, speed, and formality.
- What the learner must understand and accomplish.
- Frequency, urgency, deadline, and consequence of a breakdown.
- Any secondary requirement that must remain visible.
- Available time, learner-owned materials, interests, and preferred language balance.

When several goals compete, compare importance, frequency, urgency, and current difficulty, then
let the learner confirm the current priority.

## Define Success

Write one concise goal statement and three to five current capability directions. Use observable
communication rather than a model-invented placement score:

| Capability | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Task completion | Achieves the real purpose of the exchange |
| Listening | Understands the information needed to respond |
| Interaction | Takes turns, follows up, confirms, and repairs |
| Language resources | Retrieves enough vocabulary, chunks, grammar, and pragmatic choices |
| Fluency | Speaks in workable chunks without delays that block the task |
| Intelligibility | Uses pronunciation, stress, and rhythm that are easy enough to understand |
| Naturalness | Chooses wording and register that fit the relationship and scene |

These directions may develop at different rates. Accent imitation is not the overall goal.

## Establish A Provisional Starting Point

The first Skill invocation is a text setup, not a spoken placement test. Ask one compact set of
questions rather than a long questionnaire:

- What should the teacher call the learner? An English name is optional.
- What can the learner currently do in reading, writing, listening, and speaking, and where do they
  feel blocked?
- What result do they want, and in which real situations will they use English?
- What study rhythm is realistically sustainable, and what useful material do they already own?

Treat the answers as self-report. Do not ask the learner to enable a microphone, open GPT Live,
read aloud, or complete an audio task in the current text conversation. Record the starting point
as provisional and explicitly leave listening, speaking, pronunciation, and real-time interaction
unverified.

During the first later GPT Live class, collect short evidence naturally through the prepared
lesson:

1. Interpretive: understand the purpose or one key detail in a short natural spoken message.
2. Interpersonal: exchange information for several turns and create a chance to follow up,
   confirm, or request clarification.
3. Presentational: describe, narrate, explain, or express an opinion briefly.

Observe what the learner actually does. One self-introduction is not a placement test. Save only
representative observations and errors, update the provisional picture, and do not save raw audio
by default.

## Progress Evidence

Use this task-specific progression:

```text
supported completion
-> independent completion in a familiar task
-> independent completion with a meaningful variation
-> delayed retrieval and transfer to a related task
```

Exposure, explanation, or correct imitation alone is not mastery.
