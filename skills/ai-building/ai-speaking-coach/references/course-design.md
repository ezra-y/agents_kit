# Course Design

This document turns a confirmed learning goal and provisional starting point into a personal
course. It does not govern live turn-taking or one day's lesson details.

## Research The Goal

Search until there is enough reliable information to build the initial course, knowledge source,
and first lesson:

1. Identify the real tasks a successful learner must complete.
2. When an official exam, profession, or regulated task is involved, prefer current official
   requirements, modules, scoring guidance, and samples.
3. Add reliable teaching guidance and authentic public material.
4. Record the source, access date, and why it matters.
5. Convert findings into teachable decisions instead of copying source pages into the knowledge
   base.

Stop when the evidence supports a goal, capability directions, initial knowledge, a rolling course
block, and the first lesson. Do not search indefinitely.

## Build The Personal Course

Write `private/learner/course.md` from the course template. Include:

- The confirmed primary outcome and any protected secondary requirement.
- The learner's provisional starting evidence.
- Three to five current capability directions.
- Two to four active target tasks for the next block.
- One or two current bottlenecks.
- Observable success evidence.
- The kinds of listening, interaction, language, naturalness, and pronunciation work needed.
- The current material sources and knowledge gaps.

Default to an initial one-month direction when the learner gives no cycle. This is a rolling
direction, not a fixed day-by-day calendar.

## Build The Initial Knowledge

Create actual teachable items that serve the target tasks. Include useful chunks, patterns, short
dialogues, listening material, usage and naturalness notes, pronunciation cues, and approved
learner-owned material. Generated supplements are allowed after a naturalness and suitability
check.

A bundled sentence list may support the course but never dictates its order. Add missing scenarios,
connected listening, repair language, and learner-specific needs incrementally.

## Adapt From Evidence

After classes, preserve the goal while changing the route when evidence requires it:

| Evidence | Next course move |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated breakdown despite support | Reduce linguistic load and teach the missing subskill |
| Success with substantial prompting | Keep the target task and fade one support |
| Independent success in a familiar task | Add a meaningful variation |
| Independent transfer plus delayed retrieval | Advance the task or change the bottleneck |

Revisit the course when the goal or availability changes, evidence contradicts the provisional
profile, target tasks transfer reliably, or repeated lessons show no progress.
